Everything it does

A launcher, and the few things that belong beside one.

Just Zen lives in the menu bar. It has no browser of its own and no window you have to keep: a panel on the edge of your screen, a finder on ⌥Space, and a Claude window you open when you want it.

The panel

A floating strip, above every app and on every Space, holding the handful of places you move between.

Pin anything

Applications, web apps from a library of a hundred, any address you type, a document you are living in this week. Up to forty things, though six is usually the point.

Groups

Right-click anything and put it in a group. The group folds behind one icon made of what is inside it, and opens on a click.

Drop a file on it

Drag a spreadsheet onto Excel and it opens there. Drag anything onto anything else and Just Zen opens it with that app.

It moves

Drag the grip and the panel goes wherever you want it, on whichever screen. It remembers.

Empty on purpose

With nothing pinned it stands taller with a glowing ＋, because the only thing to do is add the first thing.

Out of the way

No Dock icon unless you ask for one. Closing the Claude window leaves the panel and the finder running.

⌥Space, the finder

One field over everything the machine can reach, ranked so two letters are usually enough.

Your applications

Everything in /Applications and your own Applications folder, with its real icon. Enter opens it; if it is running it comes forward.

Your documents

Spreadsheets, Word files, decks, PDFs, Markdown and text, found through Spotlight — including the ones in Google Drive, OneDrive and Proton Drive. Nothing is indexed twice.

Web apps

A hundred known apps with their icons, plus any address you type: Just Zen reads the page's own title and favicon and keeps it as yours.

Open tabs

The tabs already open in Safari or Chrome, by title. Choose one and it comes to the front, in whichever profile it belongs to.

Land inside the app

Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT and Slack open through their own links, so you arrive in the conversation rather than beside it.

⌘↵ pins it

Anything the finder can reach can go on the panel without leaving the keyboard.

Your browser stays your browser

Just Zen does not render web pages. It opens them where you already keep your logins.

The tab you already had

Going to a web app looks for a tab that already has that site open — in Safari or Chrome, whichever this Mac opens links with — and switches to it rather than opening another. macOS asks once whether Just Zen may control that browser; say no and it simply opens new tabs.

Profiles, extensions, passwords

All of them keep working, because they are your browser's, not ours. A work profile and a personal one stay exactly as they are.

Claude, notes and tasks

The one window Just Zen does have, opened from the panel or ⌥⇧Space.

Claude Code, sandboxed

Claude runs inside a macOS sandbox, scoped to the folder you choose, with Notes-only, Read-only and Full modes. It keeps its own login, separate from any other Claude Code on the machine.

Notes beside it

Markdown notes from the folder you connect, edited and previewed in the window, with Claude able to read exactly that folder and nothing else.

Tasks in lists

Tasks grouped into lists you make, dragged between them, folded away when a list is quiet. Select text anywhere in the window and ⌘⇧T makes it a task.

Keyboard

⌥SpaceFind anything, from any app
⌥⇧AAdd something to the panel
⌘⌥SpaceClaude, notes and tasks
⌘⇧A ⌘⇧TSelection to Claude, selection to tasks
⌘↵Put the highlighted result on the panel
⌘SSave the note you are editing

Try it for a week.

Pin six things. Press ⌥Space for the rest.
